Coffee Origins

Discover where coffee comes from and how each region shapes its flavor.

Coffee has traveled the world for centuries, evolving through culture, climate, and tradition. From the ancient Ethiopian highlands to the volcanic soils of Central America, each region brings its own story — and its own flavor profile — to every cup.

Terroir, History & Regional Profiles

Ethiopia

Floral, fruity, and tea-like. The ancestral cradle of specialty coffee.

The History: As the biological birthplace of Arabica coffee, wild forests in regions like Kaffa preserved heirloom varieties for thousands of years before cultivation began.

Flavor Profile: Highly complex, clean, and elegant. Expect heavy notes of jasmine, bergamot, and bright blueberry, with a light body reminiscent of fine black tea.

Climate & Rainfall: Tropical highland climate with stable temperatures averaging 60°F to 75°F. It experiences distinct dry seasons followed by heavy monsoon rains totaling 50 to 80 inches annually.

Harvest Season: Peak harvesting runs from November through February. This is followed by painstaking sun-drying workflows on elevated bamboo mesh beds.

🌍 Ethiopian Coffee

Latin America

Balanced, bright, and sweet. The backbone of global coffee production.

The History: Introduced via European trade routes in the 18th century, high-altitude volcanic estates across nations like Colombia, Guatemala, and Brazil transformed global espresso culture.

Flavor Profile: Exceptionally balanced and accessible. Celebrated for a smooth mouthfeel, crisp citrus acidity, and rich, decadent undertones of milk chocolate, caramel, and brown sugar.

Climate & Rainfall: Dominated by high-altitude volcanic soils, specialized cloud microclimates, and massive annual downpours (up to 100 inches) split by intense, bright solar drying windows.

Harvest Season: Varies smoothly across the equator. Central America crops harvest October through March, while Brazilian operations peak from May through September.

🌍 Latin American Coffee

East Africa (Kenya & Rwanda)

Vibrant, complex, and wine-like. Intense savory sweetness and acidity.

The History: Developing unique washing methodologies in the late 19th century, nations like Kenya established meticulous auctions and research labs that set industrial quality standards.

Flavor Profile: Intensely bold and savory. Marked by sharp, wine-like blackcurrant acidity, rich grapefruit brightness, and deep floral aromatics that stand out dramatically in a tasting.

Climate & Rainfall: High equatorial sunshine mixed with rich, iron-dense red volcanic loam. Farms sit up to 7,000 feet above sea level, causing chilly night cycles that lock sugars into the growing seeds.

Harvest Season: Features unique dual-harvest structures. The massive Main Crop strips happen from October through December, supplemented by smaller "Fly Crop" yields in April and May.

🌍 East African Coffee

Asia-Pacific (Sumatra & Java)

Earthy, deep, and bold. Heavy-bodied cups with striking herbal depth.

The History: Indonesian islands became major trade hubs under colonial monopolies. Unique local microclimates drove farmers to pioneer specialized, wet-hulled processing techniques to combat dense humidity.

Flavor Profile: Low-acidity with an immense, syrupy body. Known for wild, savory, and rustic characteristics showing distinct cedar wood, sweet tobacco, spice, and dark cocoa accents.

Climate & Rainfall: Brutally humid, continuous maritime tropical climate. Volcanic mountain tiers experience relentless rainy seasons, keeping ambient moisture levels near 85% year-round.

Harvest Season: Continuous picking cycles running heavily from April through September, requiring rapid mechanical seed hulling before internal mold can form in the wet air.

🌍 Asian Pacific Coffee
