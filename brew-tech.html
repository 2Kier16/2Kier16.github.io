Brew Techniques

Explore the essential brewing methods that shape every cup of coffee.

Brewing coffee is a balance of technique, timing, and understanding how water interacts with ground coffee. Whether you're mastering pour-over precision or dialing in espresso, these foundational methods help you unlock deeper flavor and consistency in every cup.

Core Brewing Methods

Pour Over

Clean, bright, and expressive. Perfect for highlighting origin flavors.

The Mechanics: Hot water is poured continuously over coffee grounds in a paper filter, pulling solubles through gravity alone while trapping heavy oils and sediment.

Flavor & Grind: Expect a bright, crisp, tea-like body with vivid fruit and floral notes. Requires a medium-coarse to medium grind (like sand) to prevent stalling.

Milk Pairing: Best enjoyed black or with a subtle splash of oat milk; heavy dairy easily overpowers its delicate acidity.

Espresso

Concentrated, intense, and complex. The foundation of lattes and cappuccinos.

The Mechanics: Highly pressurized water (9 bars) is forced through a finely packed coffee puck in 25–30 seconds, creating an intense, concentrated shot crowned with velvety crema.

Flavor & Grind: Viscous, heavy, and complex with rich layers of chocolate, caramel, and sharp acids. Requires an extremely fine grind (like powdered sugar).

Milk Pairing: Built perfectly for milk. Its concentrated nature cuts right through steamed dairy or plant milks to create flawless lattes, flat whites, and cortados.

French Press

Rich, full-bodied, and bold. Ideal for deeper, earthy flavor profiles.

The Mechanics: A full-immersion method where coffee steep variables remain perfectly uniform as the grounds sit directly in hot water before being separated by a metal mesh plunger.

Flavor & Grind: A robust, heavy, oil-rich texture featuring deep notes of roasted nuts, molasses, and cocoa. Requires a coarse grind (like sea salt) to prevent muddy sediment.

Milk Pairing: Its heavy, rustic body holds up incredibly well against heavy dairy creams, half-and-half, or dense whole milk.

🫖 Immersion Guides

Cold Brew

Smooth, low-acidity, and refreshing. Brewed slowly for a mellow taste.

The Mechanics: Time entirely replaces heat. Coarse coffee grounds steep in chilled or room-temperature water for 12 to 24 hours, keeping bitter hot-extracted acids bound to the grinds.

Flavor & Grind: Incredibly smooth, naturally sweet, and up to 60% less acidic than hot coffee, highlighting milk chocolate and brown sugar. Requires an extra coarse grind.

Milk Pairing: Phenomenal with ice, sweet creams, cold foam, syrups, and oat milk, serving as a highly adaptable base for chilled beverages.

🧊 Cold Extraction Physics

Grind Sizes

From coarse to fine, grind size controls extraction and flavor clarity.

The Mechanics: Grinding breaks the bean down to increase surface area, determining how quickly water can dissolve the flavor elements. Uniformity in particle distribution is critical.

The Flavor Spectrum: Grinding too fine stalls water, yielding hollow, over-extracted bitterness. Grinding too coarse lets water rush past, creating sour, watery under-extraction.

The Golden Rule: Coarser sizes match longer immersion times (French Press); finer sizes match fast, high-pressure extraction styles (Espresso).

🔬 The Science of Grinding

Water Ratios

Dial in strength and balance with proper coffee-to-water measurements.

The Mechanics: Coffee is math. Ratios are calculated cleanly in grams rather than scoops. It balances the Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) to manage beverage concentration perfectly.

The Industry Standard: The Specialty Coffee Association points to the "Golden Ratio" of 1:15 to 1:18 (1g of coffee for every 15–18g of water) for a balanced, vibrant filter cup.

Strength Control: Tight ratios (like 1:2 for espresso) optimize thick, intense yields. Wide ratios open up complex single-origins but can become hollow if over-extended.
